Financial Methodology

WACT

Weighted Average Cost of Tokens

A method for calculating true enterprise AI inference cost — weighted by workforce distribution across data classification tiers, not a flat per-seat assumption. Adapted from the logic of Weighted Average Cost of Capital.

01 — The Problem

Cost and governance are treated as separate decisions

Most enterprise AI strategy conversations split into two unconnected tracks. One team optimizes for token cost. Another team governs where data is allowed to go. They rarely model the same number.

Cost Lens

What does inference cost at scale? Token pricing, model selection, volume — modeled in isolation from data sensitivity.

Governance Lens

Where is data permitted to go? Classification tiers, custody requirements, regulatory exposure — modeled without a cost output.

WACT exists to collapse these into one number. Your data classification policy already determines which model tier and which deployment architecture each segment of your workforce is permitted to use. That constraint has a cost. WACT calculates it.

02 — The Analogy

Borrowed logic from capital structure

Weighted Average Cost of Capital blends the cost of equity and debt, weighted by their proportion of total capital. WACT applies the same blending logic to inference cost — but the weights are workforce headcount by data tier, and the "capital sources" are OpEx (API pricing) and CapEx (owned infrastructure).

WACC (Capital Structure)
WACT (Token Economics)
Cost of Equity
OpEx token rate (hyperscale API pricing)
Cost of Debt
CapEx-amortized rate (owned private/edge hardware)
Weight = proportion of capital structure
Weight = proportion of workforce in each data tier
Tax shield on debt interest
Depreciation shield on hardware CapEx (Section 179 / bonus depreciation)
03 — The Formula

Built in two steps

First, calculate the weighted token cost within each classification tier. Then sum across all five tiers and divide by total headcount.

Per Tier (n)
Tiern_Cost = (input_ratio × input_rate + output_ratio × output_rate) × tokens_per_employee × tier_employees

The output is cost per 1M tokens, per employee, weighted across the full enterprise — not a vendor benchmark, and not a flat assumption. It reflects your organization's actual data tier distribution.

04 — The Tier Model

Token ratio and deployment by classification

Each tier carries a distinct input/output ratio, reflecting how that workforce segment actually uses inference — and a distinct deployment constraint, reflecting what's legally and contractually permissible.

TierRatio (in/out)Workload CharacterRate Basis
1Public 30 / 70 General queries, no context depth OpEx — any provider
2Internal 30 / 70 Operational comms, lightweight outputs OpEx — any provider
3Confidential 30 / 70 Knowledge work — generates assets, deliverables OpEx (conditional cloud) or CapEx (private/edge)
4Restricted 50 / 50 Agent / automation — SaaS tool orchestration CapEx only — open-weight models
5Strictly Confidential 70 / 30 RAG-heavy — contracts, patents, code repos CapEx only — open-weight models
Why T4 and T5 use a different rate basis

Frontier closed-weight models — Claude, GPT-5, Gemini — are not self-hostable. They exist only as a hosted API. At Tiers 4 and 5, hyperscale cloud is not a permissible deployment regardless of contract terms, which means the only available models are open-weight (Llama, Mistral, and comparable architectures) running on infrastructure your organization owns. The cost structure shifts entirely from per-token OpEx to amortized CapEx.

05 — CapEx Methodology

Pricing inference where there is no API rate

For Tier 4 and Tier 5, the per-token rate is derived from hardware acquisition cost, amortized over useful life and effective throughput — not quoted from a vendor pricing page.

Formula

CapEx Rate per Token
Hardware_Cost CapEx_Rate = ───────────────────────────────────────────── Useful_Life_sec × Utilization_Rate × Throughput

Reference Inputs

NVIDIA H100 — per unit~$30,000
H100 cluster — minimum viable (8-GPU)~$240,000
Useful life (conservative)2–3 years
Enterprise utilization rate (typical)40–60%
Throughput — 70B model, FP8, per GPU~2,000–3,000 tok/sec
The depreciation shield

Section 179 and bonus depreciation allow qualifying hardware to be expensed immediately in Year 1 under current U.S. tax law. This materially lowers the effective cost of CapEx deployment relative to its sticker price — the same way a tax-deductible debt shield lowers the effective cost of debt in WACC. For a multi-year cloud OpEx commitment versus a depreciable hardware purchase, this shield changes which option is actually cheaper on a net present value basis.

06 — Worked Example

A 10,000-employee organization

Illustrative only. Workforce distribution, token volume, and hardware utilization vary by organization — this walks the mechanics, not a universal benchmark.

1
Establish workforce distribution by tier

Assume a financial services profile: 30% T1/T2, 55% T3, 10% T4, 5% T5.

3,000 / 5,500 / 1,000 / 500 employees
2
Apply token volume assumption

500,000 tokens per employee per month across all tiers.

3
Calculate T3 cost — the largest segment

5,500 employees, 30/70 ratio, Claude Sonnet 4.6 rates ($3 / $15 per 1M) — blended rate $11.40/1M tokens.

5,500 × 500,000 tokens × $11.40 / 1M ≈ $31,350 / mo
4
Calculate T4/T5 cost — CapEx basis

1,500 employees combined. An 8-GPU H100 cluster ($240K, 2.5-year life, 50% utilization, ~20,000 tok/sec) amortizes to roughly $0.30/1M tokens — a fraction of OpEx pricing once hardware is owned and utilized.

1,500 employees × 500,000 tokens × $0.30 / 1M ≈ $228 / mo combined
5
Sum total cost, divide by total tokens

Total monthly cost across all tiers (~$37,300) divided by total monthly tokens (5,000M) produces the enterprise WACT. A law firm or biotech with a heavier T4/T5 population will produce a materially lower figure — more of the workforce sits on near-zero CapEx-amortized cost instead of OpEx pricing.

Illustrative Enterprise WACT $7.46 / 1M tokens

Rates move — the methodology does not

OpenAI reduced GPT-5 and GPT-5 Mini input pricing by roughly 50% between April and August 2026. Anthropic and Google rates held flat over the same period. This is why WACT is expressed as a formula rather than a fixed figure: the blended rate column is an input you refresh, not a constant. Batch processing (50% discount) and prompt caching (up to 90% off cached input) reduce effective rates further and should be modeled separately where applicable.

08 — Assumptions & Limitations

Where this methodology needs your inputs

WACT is a framework, not a fixed answer. The figures above are illustrative. An accurate WACT for your organization requires your own data.

Workforce distribution
Varies significantly by industry — retail skews T1/T2, law and biotech skew T4/T5.
Token volume per employee
500K/month is a mid-range planning assumption, not a measured figure. AI copilot maturity changes this quickly.
Token ratio by tier
30/70, 50/50, 70/30 reflect typical workload archetypes — Practitioner, Orchestrator, Researcher — not a universal law.
Hardware utilization
The single most sensitive input in the CapEx model. Low utilization erases the cost advantage of owned infrastructure entirely.
Tax treatment
Depreciation shield assumes a tax-paying U.S. entity at standard corporate rates. International and non-profit structures differ.
Model pricing
OpEx rates reflect publicly posted API pricing as of the publish date and are subject to change without notice.
